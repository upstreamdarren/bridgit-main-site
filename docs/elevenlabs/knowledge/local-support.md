# Bridgit for Local Authorities and Neighbourhood Partnerships — approved sales knowledge

## The offer

Bridgit provides an approachable digital front door to preventative local support. Residents can explain what is happening in their own words and receive personalised, approved guidance across connected needs. Coaches can connect them to council, community and VCSE pathways, stay alongside them through plans and check-ins, and make a consented warm handover when a real person is needed.

The model protects human capacity. It handles early guidance, navigation and routine follow-up while specialist teams retain responsibility for judgement, safeguarding, statutory decisions and complex support.

## Problems local partnerships are trying to solve

- Residents experience one connected life, while support is divided across departments, directories and organisations.
- Housing, money, caring, health, work and isolation can intensify one another.
- People may not know where to begin and often repeat their story to several services.
- Manageable problems can become urgent and more expensive when support arrives late.
- Councils and partners need a clearer shared view of demand, engagement, pathway use, gaps and preventative outcomes.

## How the model works

1. Open a simple front door through phone, WhatsApp, a web widget, hosted link, QR code or the fuller advice-and-coaching app.
2. Understand the connected situation using clear conversational questions.
3. Give approved practical guidance and activate council, VCSE and neighbourhood pathways.
4. Keep useful context and, with consent, make a warm handover when professional support is required.

Exact channels, integrations, data flows and escalation rules are designed with each commissioning organisation.

## Value for a local authority or partnership

- Identify emerging need earlier.
- Offer useful guidance outside office hours.
- Reduce avoidable navigation contact and repetitive first-line questions.
- Connect residents to trusted council, community and VCSE support.
- Understand demand and service gaps across places and communities.
- Start with a focused, governable use case and expand after value is demonstrated.

## Reference models

Brum Chat shows how personalised guidance can connect social care, housing, money, wellbeing, work and local services. Warm Welcome partners can promote the service and use a partner portal to understand engagement and impact. Brum Chat is a reference model, not a fixed template for every council.

In Peterborough, council leadership combined digital carer support with frontline presentations, community champions, radio and multilingual accessible material. The published case study reports a 10% increase in previously unidentified unpaid carers being supported within a few months.

Bridgit reports more than 200,000 people supported across 40 local-authority areas. These examples do not guarantee identical outcomes in another place.
